According to the Massachusetts Health Insurance Survey 2023, about one in eight residents have family medical debt, and most say it arose while they were insured, pointing to deductibles, coinsurance, and billing problems rather than lack of coverage [1]. In court records, medical debts often appear as general consumer cases because they may be filed by providers, debt buyers, or medical credit card issuers, so the medical nature is hidden unless we reconstruct it from party names and docket text [2]. Since the CFPB’s 2025 rule removes medical bills from credit reports, creditors lose a key leverage point in collections and may rely more on lawsuits, making it increasingly important to examine how medical debts are pursued in Massachusetts courts [3]. Other reports also highlight how hospitals and debt buyers use aggressive collection tactics that are often hidden from the public [4].
